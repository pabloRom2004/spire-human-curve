Scope
  Human player distributions use the public replay listing, joined by run hash
  to the daily per-version bulk exports for seed IDs, solo-player checks and
  complete final item identifiers. The human cohort is replay uploaders with
  identified public accounts, not a census of all uploaded histories or owners.
  The separate seed table uses the broader uploaded run corpus.
  This is observational, self-selected upload data.

  Data-quality finding: the static export has missing player_token values even
  for known public player records. This was directly checked by matching run
  hashes against the live run listing (see identity-crosscheck-list.json).
  Therefore null bulk-export identifiers must NOT be assumed to mean genuinely
  anonymous people. Only the current public replay listing's stable player_token
  is used to attribute a run to an account. Unattributed runs remain in the seed
  table but cannot be counted as distinct players or linked by guesses.

  The matching chart uses v0.107.1, the game patch frozen by Epoch. Human seeds
  still differ from the benchmark seeds. The broader seed/aggregate view pools all 39 valid
  patch files in the manifest (2,768,051 exported rows before filtering). The
  complete corpus manifest contains 2,768,599 rows; 548 rows without a valid
  published patch file are outside this pooled dataset. Each downloaded patch
  is checked against the manifest's compressed-file SHA256 and line count.
  Only records present in both the raw snapshot and collected public replay
  listing enter a human comparison. Listing collection is paginated while the
  site is live; overlapping hashes are deduplicated and submissions after its
  start cutoff are excluded. Deletions or visibility changes during collection
  can still affect coverage; this is a collected snapshot, not an immutable
  server-side census. Keep replay-collection-state.json for timing and counts.

Eligibility
  Solo = exactly one player in the raw players list.
  Standard mode, Ascension 10, no custom modifiers, one of the five characters
  evaluated by Epoch: Ironclad, Silent, Defect, Necrobinder, Regent.
  Exclude obvious mod namespaces in final deck, relic or potion identifiers
  (hyphens or a nonstandard identifier structure). This catches visible modded
  items but cannot verify that every other gameplay mod was disabled. The raw
  export has no full enabled-mod list. Hidden/deleted records are excluded by
  the publisher's exporter. No independent anti-cheat verification is claimed.
  Unique exported run hashes are counted once per patch. Abandoned records stay
  in the primary denominator; the publisher's win flag determines the outcome.
  One A10 source row has both win and abandoned flags set; this is retained
  as reported, and disappears when excluding abandoned runs.

Player distribution and ranking
  Original static analysis: accounts with at least 20 eligible identified A10
  runs. The interactive viewer defaults to 5+ runs per account. A player's
  recorded win rate = total eligible wins / total eligible runs. Players, rather
  than runs, have equal weight in the histogram and percentile calculation.
  "Players below model" is the percentage whose recorded rate is strictly below
  the model's point estimate. Ties are reported separately in the results table.
  A zero-win model beats no zero-win players. A tie midpoint is also exported.

  These are descriptive rankings against recorded histories, not identified
  rankings of latent human ability. A short history is noisy; strategies,
  attention, experience and game patches change over time. Completion/upload
  practices can be selective. Neither the completeness nor humanity of every
  account can be independently verified. Accounts are the unit, not verified
  unique people. Renames are handled by the stable token, separate accounts
  cannot be merged. Missing records are never coded as losses.

  The curve is a reflected Gaussian kernel smoothing (bandwidth 4 percentage
  points) of observed player rates. It is not a fitted normal distribution or
  an assumed population bell curve. Histogram bins have width 5 percentage
  points. Any apparent peak or skew reflects this selected observed dataset.

Sensitivity analyses in analysis-results.json
  Minimum uploaded runs: 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, 75 and 100.
  The viewer also offers All (1+ runs) for identified replay accounts. Its
  separate All runs view includes all 439,515 retained solo standard A10
  records, including unattributed runs and visible modded items. Each run
  contributes equally to the aggregate; no player percentile is inferred.
  It covers the original five characters and official A10, across all patches,
  rather than combining other ascensions or multiplayer into this benchmark.
  Equal weight to all five characters, requiring 5, 10 or 15 runs on EACH.
  Exclude abandoned runs (can inflate rates, so not the primary definition).
  First observed attempt per account, patch, character and seed, ordered by raw
  run start time with run hash as a deterministic tie-breaker. This does not
  establish that an earlier unuploaded attempt never happened.

  Epoch weights its five characters equally (15 runs each). Primary human
  records reflect each player's chosen character mix; use the balanced analyses
  to assess this confound. Character-specialist players are absent from the
  balanced cohort, introducing a further selection difference.

Model rates and uncertainty
  The November 17 Epoch benchmarks.csv reports GPT-6.2 Astra at 37/75
  (49.3333%), GPT-6.1 Terra at 26/75 (34.6667%), and Fable 5.2 at 13/75
  (17.3333%, completed run). All displayed StS2 models now have 75 trials.
  The November 13 snapshot contained a partial Fable 5.2 result, 13/74;
  this is superseded by the completed published result, rather than an
  assumption about a missing outcome. For nonzero scores the denominators
  are cross-checked against 1 + p*(1-p)/SE^2 in the current feed.
  GPT-6.2 Sol has no published SpireClimb row in this snapshot; missing
  results are not shown as zero. Both new GPT-6.2 Astra rows use task version
  0.3.3_cd56aef, matching the existing model results.
  Original seven model rates are also confirmed by the current CSV.
  The user-supplied Epoch figure reports 75 attempts per model and rounded rates.
  The unique integer win counts consistent with those rates are 26, 22, 10, 5,
  3, 2 and 0 respectively for GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Sol, Opus 5.5,
  Fable 5.1, Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna. These are reconstructed from the figure,
  not separately obtained model trajectories. The exact fractions are used.
  The chart intervals are recomputed 90% Wilson binomial intervals. They need
  not exactly reproduce Epoch's interval procedure. Per-character rates can
  differ, so a pooled binomial interval is an approximation. The same fixed
  seeds across models also create dependence between model results.
  rank_at_model_lower/upper maps those win-rate interval endpoints onto the
  observed human distribution; it is NOT a calibrated confidence interval for
  an all-player percentile or for latent skill ranks.

Why this cannot identify a percentile among all players
  Installing a tracking mod, uploading histories and playing A10 select for
  engagement and probably experience. Their prevalence and their relationship
  to skill are unknown. The complete all-owner ability distribution is missing.
  In particular, a person who never played A10 does not have a measured A10
  probability of zero; that counterfactual is unobserved.

  Let q be the share of all players represented by an uploader-like cohort and
  F be a model's percentile within it. Only under the additional assumption
  that EVERY other player is below the model is its all-player percentile
  1 - q * (1 - F). Both q and the outsider ranking are unknown. With F=90% and
  q=1%, the formula would give the 99.9th percentile (top 0.1%), but that is an
  illustration, not an estimate. With q=10%, it instead gives top 1%.
  An elite reference win rate (e.g. 95%) does not specify a human percentile.
  0.000001% is one in 100 million; the replay dataset does not establish that
  rarity. It also cannot establish the fraction of theoretically solvable seeds.
